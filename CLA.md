# Contributor Licence Agreement

> **This is a working draft, not reviewed by a lawyer.** It is modelled on the widely used Apache
> Individual Contributor Licence Agreement with an added relicensing grant. Before relying on it —
> in particular before offering any commercial licence of this project — have a solicitor or
> attorney review it, or generate a formal equivalent at
> [harmonyagreements.org](https://www.harmonyagreements.org). The version you are asked to sign will
> be whatever this file says at the time you sign it.

## Why this exists

Kelvin is licensed to you under the **AGPL-3.0** (see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)). That means nobody —
including a well-funded competitor — can take this work, close it, and ship it. That protection is
the point.

It also means the maintainer needs to be able to license the project under *other* terms in future:
to offer a commercial edition, to distribute through the Mac App Store (whose terms the FSF considers
incompatible with the GPL family), or to relicense the project entirely. As sole copyright holder in
their own code, the maintainer can already do that. **The moment a contribution is merged, they
cannot** — not without asking every contributor for permission, which in practice means the option is
gone forever.

This agreement keeps that option open. It does **not** take your copyright away.

7. **You will say if something is not yours.** If your contribution includes third-party code, or

## What the maintainer agrees to in return

- **Every published release stays AGPL-3.0.** Nothing here allows retroactively withdrawing a
  version that has already been released as open source. If a commercial edition ever exists, it
  exists *alongside* the open one, not instead of it.
- **Your contribution keeps your name on it.** Git history is authorship, and it is not rewritten to
  remove attribution.

## How to sign

The first time you open a pull request, an automated check will ask you to accept this agreement.
Signing is a single click, recorded against your GitHub account, and you never have to do it again.

If you would prefer not to sign, that is entirely reasonable — and **you can still help**. Bug
reports, reproductions, measurements, design critique and documentation issues carry no licensing
question at all, and on a project like this they are frequently more valuable than a patch.

## Small fixes

Typo corrections, whitespace, comment fixes and other changes with no creative content do not require
a signature. The maintainer will say so on the pull request if that applies.
